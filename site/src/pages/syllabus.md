<div class="formula"><b>MINOR-PHYSICS COURSE · Semester VII</b><br/>
<b>MINOR-VII: PHYS7021 — Electricity, Magnetism and Wave Optics</b><br/>
Credits: Theory-03, Practicals-01<br/>
F.M. = 75 (Theory − 40, Practical − 20, Internal Assessment − 15)<br/>
Internal Assessment: Class Attendance (Theory) − 05 · Theory (Class Test / Assignment / Tutorial) − 05 · Practical (Sessional Viva-voce) − 05</div>

<div class="callout"><b>Course objective.</b> To provide an in-depth understanding of the principles of Electricity, Magnetism and waves, and to apply them to solve problems related to Electricity and Magnetism.</div>

<h2>Theory: 45 Hours</h2>

<div class="syl-unit" id="u1">
<div class="syl-head"><b>Unit 1 · Electrostatics</b><span class="stars" title="PYQ weightage">★★★★★★★★★★</span><span class="hrs">9 Hours</span></div>
<p><b>Electrostatic field, electric flux, Gauss's theorem of electrostatics.</b> Applications of Gauss theorem — electric field due to point charge, infinite line of charge, uniformly charged spherical shell and solid sphere, plane charged sheet, charged conductor. <span class="hrs">3 Hours</span></p>
<p><b>Electric potential as line integral of electric field</b>, potential due to a point charge, electric dipole, uniformly charged spherical shell and solid sphere. Calculation of electric field from potential. <span class="hrs">3 Hours</span></p>
<p><b>Capacitance</b> of an isolated spherical conductor. Parallel plate, spherical and cylindrical condenser. Energy per unit volume in electrostatic field. <b>Dielectric medium, polarization, displacement vector.</b> Gauss's theorem in dielectrics. Parallel plate capacitor completely filled with dielectric. <span class="hrs">3 Hours</span></p>

<h2>How to read the ★ ratings</h2>

<div class="callout"><b>★1–10 PYQ weightage.</b> Each unit is rated by how often its topics were asked in the 2018–2024 past papers (counting every appearance, including repeats). ★10 = asked almost every paper (Electrostatics — Gauss theorem alone is ×4); ★2 = rare but not absent. Ratings guide revision order, not syllabus importance — the examiner can ask anything above.</div>
